Refurbished vs new tech: is refurbished worth it?

What 'refurbished', 'renewed', and 'open box' really mean, where to buy safely, and which devices are the best refurbished value.

By the NeverShips team · Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Refurbished tech can save you a meaningful amount of money on phones, laptops, tablets, and more. It can also be a gamble if you buy from the wrong place. The difference comes down to who did the refurbishing and what warranty comes with it.

What the labels mean

  • Manufacturer refurbished / certified refurbished: returned or lightly used devices inspected, repaired if needed, and tested by the manufacturer or an authorized partner. Usually sold with a warranty similar to new and often with a new battery or outer shell.
  • Retailer or marketplace refurbished ("renewed"): refurbished by a third-party seller under the marketplace's program. Quality varies by seller. Look for a clear warranty and a return window.
  • Open box: an item that was returned, sometimes unused. Often sold by a big retailer with a full or slightly reduced warranty. Can be excellent value.
  • Used / pre-owned: sold as-is or with minimal testing. Cheapest, riskiest.

The pros

  • Price: savings of 15 to 40 percent are common, more on older models.
  • Warranty: certified refurbished devices often include the same kind of warranty as new ones.
  • Less waste: buying refurbished keeps working devices in use and out of landfills.
  • Higher tier for the same money: a refurbished higher-spec model can beat a new budget one.

The cons

  • Battery wear: batteries degrade with use. Check whether the battery was replaced or what minimum health is guaranteed.
  • Cosmetic marks: grading systems vary. "Good" at one seller may mean visible scratches.
  • Missing accessories: chargers, cables, and boxes may not be included.
  • Shorter software support: an older model may stop getting updates sooner than a new one.

How to buy refurbished safely

  1. Prefer the manufacturer's own refurbished store or a major retailer's certified program.
  2. Check the warranty length and who honors it. Ninety days is a minimum; a year is better.
  3. Check the return window and test the device thoroughly as soon as it arrives.
  4. Read the grading definitions rather than relying on the label alone.
  5. For phones, confirm it's unlocked or compatible with your carrier, and that it isn't reported lost or stolen.
  6. Check the software support timeline for that model.

What to test on arrival

  • Screen: look for dead pixels, burn-in, and touch dead spots.
  • Battery: check the reported battery health in settings, if available.
  • Ports, buttons, speakers, microphones, and cameras.
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular connections.
  • That the device isn't still locked to a previous owner's account.

Best and worst categories for refurbished

Laptops, tablets, phones, monitors, and game consoles are usually good refurbished buys from reputable sellers. Items where wear really matters, like cheap earbuds, or items that are inexpensive new anyway, are rarely worth the risk. Whatever you buy, a warranty matters more than the discount.
