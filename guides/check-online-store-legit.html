How to check if an online store is legit

A practical checklist for vetting unfamiliar shopping sites: domain age, contact details, payment options, reviews, and policies.

By the NeverShips team · Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Social media ads, search results, and price comparison sites regularly surface stores you've never heard of. Many are perfectly fine small businesses. Some are not. This checklist helps you tell the difference in about five minutes.

Check the address bar first

  • Spelling. Read the domain slowly. Look-alike domains swap letters (rn for m), add words, or use a brand name with an unusual ending.
  • HTTPS isn't proof. The padlock only means the connection is encrypted. Scam sites get certificates too, and they're free. A missing padlock on a checkout page is a bad sign, but its presence doesn't prove anything.
  • Domain age. Use a WHOIS lookup to see when the domain was registered. A brand-new domain selling high-value items at big discounts deserves extra caution.

Look for real contact information

A legitimate store makes it easy to reach a human. Look for:

  • A physical business address you can search on a map.
  • A phone number or live chat that someone actually answers.
  • An email address on the store's own domain, not a free webmail account.
  • A company name you can look up in a business registry.

Try sending a pre-sale question. How quickly, and how sensibly, they reply tells you a lot.

Read the policies

Open the return, refund, shipping, and privacy pages. Real stores write these for their own business. Scam sites often copy them from elsewhere, so look for another company's name, mismatched countries, or policies that contradict each other. Pay attention to shipping times: "ships in 15 to 30 business days" from a store claiming to be local is a warning.

Check outside reviews

Ignore the testimonials on the store's own site. Instead:

  • Search the store name with "reviews," "scam," and "complaints."
  • Look at independent review platforms and community forums.
  • Check whether the store has a long-running social media presence with real customer interaction, not just ads.
  • Be suspicious of a sudden burst of very similar five-star reviews.

Look at the payment options

Stores that accept major credit cards through a normal checkout, or well-known payment services with buyer protection, give you a way to recover your money if something goes wrong. A store that pushes bank transfers, gift cards, crypto, or payment-app transfers to an individual is a store to avoid.

Watch the product pages

  • Photos lifted straight from the manufacturer with no original images.
  • Every item discounted by the same huge percentage.
  • Spelling and grammar that suddenly changes between pages.
  • Unrealistic stock: dozens of sold-out items "back in stock" only on this site.

Use a tool as a second opinion

Browser safe-browsing warnings and website reputation checkers can flag known scam sites. They're useful but not complete, since brand-new scam sites may not be listed yet. Treat a clean result as "no known problems," not a guarantee.

When in doubt

Buy the same item from a retailer you already trust, even if it costs a little more. The extra few dollars buy you a real return policy and a package that actually arrives. For a quick version of this checklist, see how to spot a fake tech deal.
