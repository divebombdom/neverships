The 48-hour rule for impulse gadget buys

A simple waiting rule that separates real wants from late-night cart regret, plus questions to ask before any tech upgrade.

By the NeverShips team · Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Tech is one of the easiest categories to impulse buy. New products are announced constantly, reviews make every upgrade sound essential, and one-click checkout removes the last bit of friction. The 48-hour rule puts that friction back.

How the rule works

When you want something non-essential, put it in your cart or on a list, then wait 48 hours before buying. That's it. You aren't saying no. You're saying "not right now."

  • If you still want it after two days, you've confirmed it's a genuine want, and you've had time to compare prices and read reviews from more than one source.
  • If you forgot about it, you just saved the money without any willpower at all.

Most impulse purchases are driven by a short spike of excitement. That feeling fades quickly, and what's left is a clearer sense of whether the thing actually fits your life.

Questions to ask during the wait

What does it replace?

If the answer is "a perfectly good device I already own," the upgrade is probably smaller than it feels. Look up a direct comparison between your current model and the new one, focusing on the features you actually use.

What problem does it solve?

Be specific. "My laptop takes four minutes to export a video and I do that daily" is a real problem. "The new one looks nicer" is a want, which is fine, but worth naming honestly.

Is a new model coming soon?

Many product lines refresh on predictable schedules. Buying a month before a new version launches is one of the most common tech regrets, because the old model often drops in price right after. Our guide on the best time to buy tech covers typical cycles.

Would refurbished do?

Certified refurbished devices from the manufacturer or a major retailer can save a lot with little downside. See refurbished vs new.

What's the total cost?

Add cases, chargers, accessories, subscriptions, and any warranty. A $300 gadget that needs $120 of extras is a $420 decision.

Make the wait easier

  • Remove saved payment details from shopping sites so buying takes a few extra steps.
  • Unsubscribe from deal emails or filter them into a folder you check weekly.
  • Use a price tracker so you're notified if the price drops while you wait.
  • Keep a wish list and review it monthly. You'll notice how many items no longer appeal.

When not to wait

The rule is for wants, not needs. If your only laptop died and you work from home, buy a sensible replacement. If a genuine, verified sale on something you've wanted for months ends tonight, the waiting already happened. The goal isn't to never buy things. It's to buy things you'll still be glad you bought next month.
