What to do before you sell or trade in your old phone

Back up, sign out, remove locks, wipe, and get the best price, a step-by-step checklist for selling or trading in a phone safely.

By the NeverShips team · Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Selling or trading in an old phone is a great way to cut the cost of a new one. But your phone holds a lot of personal information, and skipping a step can leave your data exposed or leave the buyer with a locked, useless device. Follow this checklist in order.

1. Back everything up

  • Run a full backup to your cloud account or computer.
  • Check that photos and videos have finished syncing.
  • Make sure messaging apps have their own chat backups turned on, since some store chats separately.
  • Save anything stored only on the device, like voice memos or downloaded files.

2. Move your two-factor authentication

This is the step people most often forget. If you use an authenticator app for two-factor codes, transfer those accounts to your new phone before wiping the old one, or make sure you have backup codes. Otherwise you could lock yourself out of important accounts.

3. Set up the new phone first

If possible, transfer your data to the new phone and confirm everything works, including banking apps and messaging, before erasing the old one.

4. Unpair accessories and remove cards

  • Unpair smartwatches and earbuds.
  • Remove payment cards from your mobile wallet.
  • Take out the SIM card and any memory card. If you use an eSIM, follow your carrier's steps to move or remove it.

5. Sign out of your accounts and turn off anti-theft locks

Modern phones have activation locks tied to your account (such as Find My on iPhone or Factory Reset Protection on Android). If you don't sign out of your account before resetting, the next owner may not be able to set up the phone. Sign out of your main account in settings, which typically turns off the lock.

6. Factory reset

Use the phone's built-in "erase all content and settings" or "factory reset" option. Modern phones encrypt storage by default, so a proper reset makes the old data unreadable. After the reset, the phone should show the initial setup screen.

7. Remove it from your account online

Log in to your account on the web and remove the old device from your trusted devices list. If you used a device-finding service, remove the phone there too.

8. Get the best price

  • Compare offers: manufacturer trade-in, carrier trade-in, retailer trade-in, buyback services, and private sale often differ significantly.
  • Clean it and photograph it honestly if you're selling privately.
  • Include the box and charger if you still have them.
  • Grade it accurately. Buyback services will re-check the condition and lower the offer if it doesn't match.

9. Sell safely

When selling privately, meet in a public place, ideally one designated for online sales, and accept payment methods that can't be reversed after you hand over the phone. Be wary of buyers who overpay and ask for a refund of the difference, or who want to ship it somewhere far away before paying. Those are common scams. The same instincts that help you vet an online store apply here.

10. Or recycle it

If the phone is too old to sell, many manufacturers, carriers, and electronics retailers take old devices for free recycling. Wipe it first using the same steps above.
