When is the best time to buy tech?

How product release cycles and sales seasons affect prices on phones, laptops, TVs, and consoles, and how to use price trackers.

By the NeverShips team · Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Timing a tech purchase well can save more than any coupon. Prices on most electronics follow two patterns: product release cycles and retail sales seasons. Understanding both helps you avoid buying at the peak.

Pattern 1: release cycles

Most major product lines get refreshed on a fairly regular schedule, often yearly for phones and many laptops, and every few years for consoles and graphics cards. The timing matters in two ways:

  • Right before a launch is often the worst time to buy the current model at full price, because it may soon be discounted or replaced.
  • Right after a launch is often a good time to buy the previous model, as retailers clear stock.

To check where a product is in its cycle, look up when the current model was released and when its predecessors launched. Reputable tech news sites often track expected release windows, though rumors aren't guarantees.

Pattern 2: sales seasons

Retail sales tend to cluster around the same periods every year. In the US, the biggest are:

  • Late November (Black Friday and Cyber Monday): broad discounts across nearly every category, especially TVs, headphones, smart home devices, and laptops.
  • Back-to-school (roughly July to September): laptops, tablets, and accessories, often with student discounts.
  • Major retailer sale events: large online retailers run their own multi-day sales, often in summer and autumn.
  • After the holidays: some clearance in January, and TV deals ahead of big sporting events.

Is the sale price actually a deal?

Not every "sale" is real. Some retailers inflate the original price so the discount looks bigger. Price trackers solve this by showing the price history of a product over months. If the "deal" price matches what it has cost most of the year, it isn't much of a deal. Many trackers also let you set an alert for a target price, which pairs well with the 48-hour rule.

Category tips

Phones

Previous-generation models usually drop after the new launch. Carrier deals can look great but often lock you into long contracts. Compare the total cost over the contract against buying outright.

Laptops

Back-to-school and late-November sales are typically strong. Make sure discounted models aren't older chips being cleared at prices that are still too high for their age. See laptop specs that actually matter.

TVs

New lineups are usually announced early in the year and arrive in spring, so the previous year's models are commonly discounted heavily in the run-up to and during late-autumn sales.

Consoles and graphics cards

Launch periods often bring shortages and scalping. Patience usually wins: stock and bundles improve over time. Never buy from a stranger asking for gift cards or wire transfers.

The best time is when you need it

If your device has died and you rely on it, waiting months for a sale isn't worth the disruption. Buy a sensible option, consider refurbished to save money, and keep your receipt in case of a price drop. Some retailers offer price adjustments within a short window.
