Package says delivered but isn't there: what to do, step by step

A calm, ordered checklist for when tracking says delivered and your doorstep says otherwise, from the first 24 hours to card disputes.

By the NeverShips team · Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Few things are as frustrating as a tracking page that says "Delivered" when there's nothing at your door. The good news: unlike the orders on NeverShips, real packages usually do turn up, and when they don't, you generally have options. Work through these steps in order.

Step 1: Give it 24 hours

Drivers sometimes scan packages as delivered a little early, often near the end of a route, and drop them off later. Some carriers also mark items delivered when they reach a parcel locker, a mailroom, or a neighbor. Unless the item is urgent, waiting until the next day resolves a surprising number of cases.

Step 2: Search the obvious and the not-so-obvious places

  • Back door, side door, garage, patio, and behind planters or bins.
  • Your mailbox or a parcel locker, if you have one.
  • Your building's mailroom, front desk, or leasing office.
  • Neighbors' doorsteps, especially if your house number is easy to misread.
  • Anyone else in your household who might have brought it inside.

Step 3: Read the tracking details closely

Open the carrier's own tracking page, not just the store's summary. Many carriers show a delivery photo, a note like "left at front door" or "handed to resident," and sometimes a map pin. A photo of a door that isn't yours is useful evidence. Check that the delivery address on the order is correct, including apartment number.

Step 4: Contact the seller first

This surprises people, but the seller is usually your best first call. You paid the seller, not the carrier, and most large retailers will reship or refund a missing item after a short investigation. They also have far more leverage with the carrier than you do. Keep it simple: order number, tracking number, what you checked, and what you'd like (replacement or refund).

Step 5: File a carrier claim if asked

Some sellers, especially smaller ones or marketplace sellers, will ask you to open a case with the carrier. Each major carrier has an online form for missing packages; in the US, USPS calls this a Missing Mail search request. Do it promptly, since carriers have time limits for claims.

Step 6: Use the marketplace's guarantee

If you bought through a marketplace, check its buyer protection program. Many marketplaces will refund you directly if a seller doesn't resolve a missing-item report within a set number of days. Open the case through the marketplace, not by email with the seller, so it's on record.

Step 7: Dispute the charge as a last resort

If the seller won't help, contact your credit card issuer and dispute the charge for goods not received. Have your screenshots ready: order confirmation, tracking history, delivery photo, and your messages with the seller. In the US, the Fair Credit Billing Act generally gives you 60 days from the statement date to dispute a billing error on a credit card, so don't wait too long. Debit card and payment app protections vary and are often weaker.

How to prevent it next time

  • Use a parcel locker or pickup point for expensive items.
  • Require a signature on high-value orders when the option exists.
  • Turn on carrier delivery notifications so you can grab packages quickly.
  • Ship to your workplace if that's allowed and more secure.
  • Install a doorbell camera or simple lockbox if porch theft is common where you live.
