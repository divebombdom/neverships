Package tracking statuses explained

What 'label created', 'in transit', 'out for delivery', and 'delivery exception' actually mean, and when to worry.

By the NeverShips team · Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Tracking pages are written by and for logistics systems, which is why they can be so confusing. Every carrier uses slightly different wording, but most updates fall into a handful of stages. Here's what they usually mean and how long to wait before worrying. (On NeverShips, every status means the same thing: it's not coming.)

Label created / shipping information received

The seller has printed a shipping label and sent the details to the carrier. The carrier doesn't have the package yet. It's normal for an order to sit at this stage for a day or two while the seller packs it. If it stays here for more than three or four business days, contact the seller, because the package may never have been handed over.

Picked up / accepted / origin scan

The carrier now physically has the package. From here, the clock on the carrier's delivery estimate starts.

In transit / arrived at facility / departed facility

The package is moving between sorting centers. Updates might come several times a day or not for a couple of days, especially over long distances or weekends. Gaps of a day or two are normal. If there's been no update for five or more business days on a domestic shipment, contact the seller.

Seeing a facility far from you, or even a backward-looking route, isn't necessarily a problem. Carriers route packages through central hubs that may not be on the most direct path.

Out for delivery

The package is on a local delivery vehicle and should arrive that day, usually by evening. Occasionally it rolls over to the next day if the route runs long.

Delivery attempted / notice left

The driver tried to deliver but couldn't, often because a signature was required, there was no safe place to leave it, or access was blocked. Check for a paper notice. The carrier will usually try again or hold the package at a local facility for pickup. Follow the instructions on the official tracking page, never in an unexpected text message. See fake delivery texts for why.

Delivery exception / delay

Something unexpected happened: bad weather, a damaged label, an incorrect address, a holiday, or a vehicle issue. Most exceptions resolve within a day or two. If the exception mentions the address, check your order and contact the seller or carrier to correct it.

Held at facility / available for pickup

The package is waiting at a carrier location or pickup point. There's usually a deadline, often five to fifteen days, after which it's returned to the sender. Bring ID and the tracking number.

Returned to sender

The package is heading back to the seller, usually because of an address problem, a refused delivery, or an uncollected pickup. Contact the seller to arrange a reship or refund.

Delivered

The carrier says the package reached its destination. If you can't find it, work through our step-by-step guide for when a package says delivered but isn't there.

Tips for smoother tracking

  • Sign up for notifications in the carrier's official app.
  • Make sure your address includes apartment or unit numbers.
  • Remember that estimates are business days, and weekends and holidays don't count.
  • Keep the original order confirmation email until the item arrives and works.
