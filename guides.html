Shopping guides that actually help

Everything else on NeverShips is a joke. These aren't. They're practical tips for buying real tech without getting burned.

How to spot a fake tech deal

Scam storefronts and too-good-to-be-true listings tend to share the same tells. Watch for these before you pay:

  1. The price is wildly below every major retailer. A current-generation flagship phone or GPU at 70% off from an unknown site is almost never real.
  2. The domain is new or odd. Look for misspellings of known brands, extra words, or unusual endings. A quick WHOIS lookup shows how old a domain is.
  3. Payment is only by wire, gift card, crypto, or payment app. These are hard to reverse. Credit cards offer the strongest dispute protection.
  4. There's no real contact info. No physical address, no phone number, or only a web form.
  5. Countdown pressure everywhere. Fake urgency — "3 left!", timers that reset on refresh — is designed to stop you from thinking. (Yes, we use one too. Ours is a joke.)
  6. Reviews are all five stars and all sound the same. Search the store name plus "scam" or "reviews" on a third-party site.
  7. Return policy is vague or missing. Legit retailers state clear return windows.

What to do when a package says delivered but isn't there

Unlike ours, real packages usually turn up. Work through these steps in order:

  1. Wait 24 hours. Carriers sometimes mark items delivered early, at the end of a route.
  2. Check everywhere nearby. Back door, garage, mailroom, neighbors, bushes, and building front desks.
  3. Look at the delivery photo or GPS details in the carrier's tracking page, if available.
  4. Contact the seller first. Most retailers will reship or refund, and they have more leverage with the carrier than you do.
  5. File a claim with the carrier if the seller asks you to.
  6. Dispute the charge with your card issuer as a last resort, with screenshots of tracking and your communication.

The 48-hour rule for impulse gadget buys

Most tech regret comes from buying in the moment. A simple rule: when you want something non-essential, put it in your cart and wait 48 hours.

  • If you still want it, you've confirmed it's a real want, and you've had time to compare prices and read reviews.
  • If you forgot about it, you just saved the money.
  • Ask what it replaces. If the answer is "a perfectly good device I already own," the upgrade is probably marginal.
  • Check the release cycle. Many product lines refresh on a predictable schedule. Buying right before a new model drops is a common regret.
